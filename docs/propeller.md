# Propeller design report

The pedal kayak's propeller. Generated by `tools/propeller.py` (run it with `--write`); its
geometry is `prop.sv`. Every number below is computed. The assumptions are listed where they are used.

## Design basis

- **Water:** sea water at 10 °C, ρ = 1025 kg/m³, ν = 1.35e-06 m²/s, vapour pressure 1230 Pa.
- **Hull:** drag from ITTC-57 friction on 2.67 m² wetted, form factor 1.12, plus wave-making
  of 25% of friction at Froude number 0.29, growing as Fn⁴. The hull figures are `tools/lines.py`'s at 156 kg.
- **Hull interaction:** wake fraction 0.03, thrust deduction 0.03. The disc is 0.5 m behind
  the transom on the hinged tail. The shaft runs 11.3° below level, so the required
  thrust is divided by the cosine of that angle. The crossflow across the disc is the hull's speed times its sine.
- **Rider and drive:** the blade is designed at 160 W and 70 rpm, a hard sustained effort, not at the
  100 W cruise. A blade designed at 100 W runs at higher incidence when pushed, and was
  predicted to cavitate from about 185 W. Designed at 160 W it stays clear to 250 W, costing under 1 % of
  efficiency at 100 W, and the cruise cadence falls to about 61 rpm. The drive is 94% efficient; the train is 7:1.
- **Sections:** polars from NeuralFoil (an XFOIL-trained surrogate, `large` model), n_crit 5. Each
  station is read at its own Reynolds number, which runs from 3×10⁴ to 10⁵: laminar separation bubbles
  dominate the drag here, which is why the polars and not textbook drag coefficients set the sections.

## Method

1. **Trade study.** For each diameter and gear ratio, a minimum-induced-loss blade is designed at the speed it reaches.
2. **Design.** Adkins & Liebeck (1994), *Design of Optimum Propellers*: Betz's minimum induced loss with Prandtl's
   tip loss and profile drag. At every station the camber and design lift minimise drag/lift at that
   station's Reynolds number, at least 0.35 below the section's maximum lift. Smooth curves are then drawn
   through the chosen cambers and lifts, and the blade is designed again on those curves.
3. **Buildable blade.** The tip rounds parabolically from 0.9 R to 25% of the chord there. Each
   station's pitch angle is re-derived so that its section still meets its design lift.
4. **Analysis.** Blade-element momentum with Prandtl's tip and hub losses on the final blade. This
   gives every performance, strength and cavitation number below.
5. **Matching.** For each pedal power: the speed and cadence at which the propeller absorbs that power through
   the drive, and its thrust meets the hull's drag.

## Strength

- **Material:** nickel-aluminium bronze C95800. Yield 240 MPa, ultimate 585 MPa, corrosion
  fatigue in sea water 105 MPa (10⁸ cycles, reversed; a literature value for as-cast material).
- **Sprint start:** a standing start at a 130 rpm cadence (910 rpm, 0.3 m/s), 5.79 N·m, 179 N of thrust.
  Most of the blade is stalled here, and the polars past stall are NeuralFoil's least confident.
  Limit: yield / 1.5 = 160 MPa. The cranks' 200 N·m peak cannot reach the blade: a propeller's
  torque is set by its speed, and below that speed the push only spins it up.
- **Cruise fatigue:** the steady stress at 100 W, plus the alternation once a revolution from the tail's
  crossflow (±0.43 m/s, tangential at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions). Goodman, required safety factor 2.0.
- Bending is taken about each section's weak principal axis, from its own coordinates, under the
  combined thrust and tangential loads outboard of it.

The root takes the highest stress. Give it a fillet of at least 3 mm radius to the hub. The root's Goodman
factor includes a 1.3× fillet concentration. With it, the root's sprint stress is 98 MPa against the
160 MPa limit (within it).

**Strikes.** The shear pin shears at 8.3 N·m, 1.44× the sprint start's torque. A tangential
blow at 0.8 R that shears it is 77 N. That puts 75 MPa into the root, or
98 MPa with the fillet's 1.3× concentration, against 240 MPa yield. So the pin shears before the blade bends.
A blow from below or behind is the kick-up tail's to absorb.

## Cavitation and ventilation

The disc's centre is 195 mm deep at the design load (60 mm of tip cover). Each section's cavitation number
is computed at the top of the disc (shallowest) and at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions (where the crossflow
swings the incidence). It is set against a thin-section estimate of the least pressure coefficient:
thickness, load, and the leading edge's peak for incidence away from ideal.

Ventilation is the likelier nuisance. With 60 mm of tip cover (0.22 D), a steep wave or a
hard turn can draw air down to the upper blade. The guard fin under the tail and the tail's own
depth limit it. Avoid running with the tail partly raised.

## Manufacture

- **Material and process:** cast in C95800 nickel-aluminium bronze and finish-machined, or 5-axis machined
  from bar. Two other choices:
  - 17-4PH stainless steel, H1150: stronger, heavier, and needs care against crevice corrosion.
  - A 3D-printed master for an investment casting.
  Not aluminium: its corrosion fatigue in sea water is poor.
- **Geometry:** export `prop.sv`'s blades and hub (`solventc prop.sv --stl …`), or loft the section offsets above.
- **Tolerances:** adapted from ISO 484-2 Class I, scaled to this diameter.
  - Local pitch ±1.5 %; mean pitch of each blade ±1 %.
  - Section thickness +0.15 / −0.05 mm.
  - Chord ±0.5 mm.
  - Leading and trailing edges to template within 0.1 mm.
  - Blade-to-blade angular spacing ±0.5°.
- **Surface:** Ra ≤ 1.6 µm on both faces, ≤ 0.8 µm over the outer half of the back. Edges sharp to the offsets.
- **Balance:** static and dynamic, to ISO 1940 G6.3 at 650 rpm. That is a residual unbalance of about
  83 g·mm.
- **Hub:** bore Ø16 H7 on a 316 shaft. A Ø1.8 mm C36000 brass shear pin is cross-drilled through hub and
  shaft. A spinner nut and a split pin hold it aft. Fit a sacrificial zinc anode on the tail tube.

## Limits of this design

- **Polars:** NeuralFoil's, not tunnel data. At Re 3×10⁴–10⁵ the laminar-bubble behaviour is sensitive
  to free-stream turbulence (n_crit). A test of the finished propeller, thrust and torque against rpm in
  a tank or behind the boat, would anchor them.
- **Hull model:** the hull's drag and its wake and thrust deduction are estimates. The design point moves
  with them, but the efficiency curve is flat across 40–250 W, so an error moves the cadence, not the efficiency.
- **Sections:** drawn square to the stacking line, not wrapped on their cylinders. At the root a
  section's ends stand about 1 mm outboard of its radius.
- **Cavitation:** the estimate is thin-section superposition, not a panel-method pressure distribution.
